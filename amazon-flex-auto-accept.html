Amazon Flex Auto Accept: How Auto-Grabbing Blocks Works

"Auto accept" is the part of grabbing that does the final tap for you: the moment a block matches your rules, it gets accepted automatically. Here is exactly how that works in FlexPilot, which offers it takes, and the honest trade-offs before you rely on it.

What "auto accept" means on Amazon Flex

On Amazon Flex, work shows up as blocks — a warehouse, a start time, a pay amount, and a duration. Normally you refresh the offer screen, spot a block you want, and tap Accept before another driver takes it. Amazon Flex auto accept simply means software does that last tap for you: the instant a block that fits your rules appears, it gets accepted automatically instead of waiting on your thumb.

Drivers use a pile of names for the same idea. "Auto grabber," "block auto acceptor," "auto accept app," and "auto accept flex blocks" all describe the same behavior — watch the screen, and accept a matching offer without a manual tap. It is a narrower thing than a general Amazon Flex bot: auto-accept is specifically the accept-on-match step, not scheduling, account management, or the other jobs people lump under "bot."

The reason drivers want it is timing. Good blocks can vanish in seconds, so the gap between seeing an offer and tapping it is often the difference between getting the block and watching it disappear. Sitting there refreshing for an hour is exhausting, and human reaction time is the slow link. Auto-accept exists to remove that lag on the offers you already know you want.

How FlexPilot auto-accepts a matching block

FlexPilot is an Android app that runs on your own phone through Android's accessibility service. Instead of logging into your account or routing anything through outside servers, it watches the official Amazon Flex offer screen while you have it open, reads each block that appears, and — when one matches the filters you set — taps Accept in milliseconds.

That speed is the whole value. A person sees the offer, decides, then moves a thumb; the app skips the decision lag and reacts the moment a qualifying block renders. If you want the underlying mechanism spelled out, we cover it in detail in how grabbers detect and tap offers, but the short version is this: it is your phone, your logged-in Amazon Flex app, and an automated tap on the offers you already told it to take.

Because everything happens locally, FlexPilot never asks for your Amazon password and does not sit between you and Amazon. You stay signed into the official app the normal way — FlexPilot just supplies the fast, consistent tap.

Which offers it will and won't accept

Auto-accept is only useful if it grabs the right blocks and ignores the rest. FlexPilot decides that with filters you control:

  • Minimum pay — set a dollar floor so low-paying blocks get skipped.
  • Station / warehouse — accept offers only from the locations you actually want to drive from.
  • Time and day — restrict auto-accept to the windows you are available.
  • Block duration — take only blocks that fit your schedule, short or long.
  • Pay-per-hour floor — screen on effective hourly rate, not just total pay, so a long low-value block does not slip through.

Anything outside those rules is left alone. If a block pays under your minimum, starts at a warehouse you did not pick, or falls outside your hours, the app ignores it. That is the point: you are not accepting everything that appears, you are auto-accepting only the offers you would have wanted anyway.

The filters also work together rather than as loose suggestions. A block has to clear every rule you have set — pay and location and time and duration and hourly rate — before it is accepted. If it fails any one of them, it is passed over, so tightening one filter never quietly opens up another.

Human-like pacing and session control

FlexPilot includes a humanization option that adds more natural pacing to how it acts, instead of reacting in a rigid, perfectly uniform way. It is worth being clear-eyed here: pacing can make automated behavior look less mechanical, but it cannot make automation invisible, and no setting changes the underlying fact that you are automating. Anyone selling a "pacing" toggle as a shield is overselling it, and we would rather you know that going in.

You also decide when it runs. Auto-accept is start/stop — it only works while you have a session active, so you flip it on when you are ready to grab and turn it off the rest of the time. Nothing keeps running in the background accepting blocks you never asked for.

Is auto-accepting allowed? The honest answer

Here is the part a sales pitch skips. Using any automation to accept blocks — FlexPilot included — is against Amazon Flex's Terms of Service. Amazon does not permit third-party tools to interact with the Flex app, and drivers who automate can face consequences up to and including permanent deactivation of their account.

No tool can promise to keep you safe from that, and any app that claims it can is not being straight with you.
