The most common reasons Amazon Flex deactivates drivers

Most deactivations trace back to your standing metrics and a handful of policy lines. Amazon watches a set of behaviors closely, and repeated problems in any of them can move you from "Fantastic" or "Great" standing down toward "At Risk." The patterns that come up again and again include:

  • Reliability failures. Accepting a block and then not showing up, forfeiting late, or a string of missed and abandoned blocks. Amazon treats last-minute cancellations and no-shows as one of the most serious signals.
  • Late arrivals and unfinished routes. Consistently arriving after your block start time, or returning packages instead of completing deliveries, chips away at your on-time and completion rates.
  • Low delivery quality. Poor customer-delivery ratings, ignoring delivery instructions, unsafe package placement, or marking a package delivered when it was not.
  • Address and location issues. Being flagged for delivering from the wrong location, spoofing GPS, or attendance/geolocation mismatches at the station.
  • Safety and conduct. Accidents, in-app driving-behavior flags, or complaints about interactions with customers and staff.
  • Policy and eligibility violations. Background-check or identity problems, sharing an account, or anything Amazon reads as gaming the offer system.

The common thread is trust. Amazon is deciding, block by block, whether packages are safe in your hands. Most drivers who lose their account did not do one catastrophic thing; they accumulated small reliability and quality hits until the system flagged them.

If you have already been deactivated: appeal basics

If your account was deactivated, Amazon typically sends an email explaining the decision, and it usually contains a reply address or link for appeals. Read it carefully, because the stated reason shapes how you respond. When you reply, be concise, professional, and specific: acknowledge the issue if it is legitimate, or clearly explain the misunderstanding if you believe it is an error.

Documentation helps. If your case involves timing, addresses, or app problems, screenshots, GPS records, support-ticket numbers, and dates give your appeal something concrete to stand on. Keep it factual rather than emotional. That said, set realistic expectations: appeals are reviewed at Amazon's discretion, some succeed and many do not, and there is no formula that forces a reinstatement. Send your best, most honest account of what happened, then follow up politely if you do not hear back.
